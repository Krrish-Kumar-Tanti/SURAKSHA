# 1. One-page summary

## 1.1 What we are building

**SURAKSHA — AICTE Security Deposit Control Tower** (working name).

Not another FDR upload portal. A policy-native control tower that proves every rupee is:

1. **Real** — declared vs. document vs. bank, field by field.
2. **Correctly sized** — exact APH Table 1.4, 2026-27 50% concession, leased multiplier, exemptions.
3. **Continuously pledged** — maturity, renewal, lien, duplicate-pledge detection.
4. **Safe to release** — the full Clause 6.46 release eligibility graph, with clearances and human approval.
5. **Auditable** — hash-chained evidence, role-based access, time machine.

## 1.2 Why this PS

- `[FACT]` The PS has the lowest strong-competitor density of the five: 13/500 national ideas on 25 Oct 2026; 6/8 DScE team slots left.
- `[FACT]` The strongest incumbent, `Shash1704/aicte-fdr-portal`, is excellent on provenance, bank sandbox, reminders and release workflow, but its own README says requirement amounts/periods are **illustrative and editable**.
- `[FACT]` It does not encode the 2026-27 Addendum/Corrigendum: the 50% concession for NE/J&K/Ladakh/Aspirational Districts/A&N/Lakshadweep/women-only/PwD-only institutions, the leased-building/land multiplier, or RPGF in release.
- `[INFERENCE]` This is a real policy moat. Most teams will build a generic portal; we can build the compliance brain.

## 1.3 What the jury will remember

> “The institution declared ₹70 lakh. The document said ₹70 lakh. The bank said ₹35 lakh. The system showed all three, cited the clause, blocked the release, and produced a tamper-evident audit trail.”

That is a money story, a rules story, and a trust story in one demo.

## 1.4 What we will not build

- Not a bank, payment gateway, or investment manager.
- Not a generic document locker.
- Not an auto-approval or auto-penalty system.
- Not a blockchain product. We use a hash-chained audit log.
- Not a real bank/APAAR/DigiLocker integration. We use labelled adapters and a sandbox.
- Not a full OCR research project. Text-layer extraction first; OCR fallback; human review.

`[FACT]` Security deposits/FDRs are part of approval/compliance. Management involves:

- institution-wise records,
- verifying instruments,
- monitoring validity/maturity,
- tracking renewal/replacement,
- recording release/refund requests,

## 2.4 What the PS explicitly asks for

1. **Centralized repository:** institution-wise and instrument-wise records, reference numbers, issuing bank details, amount, issue date, maturity date, validity status, supporting documents, current administrative status.
2. **Digital document management:** secure upload/storage/retrieval/review of FDRs, deposit confirmations, prescribed records.
3. **Verification/discrepancy detection:** review submitted information; verify instrument details through legitimate bank-issued verification mechanisms where supported; identify incomplete records; flag discrepancies; distinguish institution-submitted vs. independently bank-confirmed information.
4. **Automated validity/maturity tracking:** maturity dates, validity periods, renewal/replacement requirements, prescribed timelines; configurable reminders before deadlines; overdue/unresolved flags.
5. **Renewal/replacement monitoring:** track submission of renewed/replacement instruments; complete history of changes.
6. **Release/refund workflow:** submission, processing and status tracking subject to applicable regulations, prescribed conditions and competent-authority authorization.
7. **Alerts/escalation:** pending verification, approaching deadlines, overdue actions, unresolved cases.
8. **Dashboard/reports:** active, expiring, matured, renewed, released, pending; search; document histories; permitted field updates; scrutiny observations; status monitoring; automated reports for pending verifications, upcoming maturities, missing documents, intervention cases.
9. **Audit trail/access control:** traceable submissions, verification results, changes, approvals, actions; RBAC; secure document storage; encryption; controlled access to financial/institutional information.
10. **Integration/data security:** where technically feasible and formally authorized, integrate with existing AICTE systems and bank verification services to reduce duplicate data entry and improve reliability.

# 4. Domain rules we must encode

> This section is the team’s unfair advantage. Most competitors will not read the APH.

## 4.0 What APH and the Addendum are (plain language)

`[FACT]` **APH** = `Approval Process Handbook 2024-25 to 2026-27`, AICTE’s official rulebook for approval, compliance and security deposits. Its cover states: “This Handbook is a Legal Document as per All India Council for Technical Education Act, 1987 (52 of 1987).” It is not a portal, a dashboard, or a research paper.

`[FACT]` For SIH26265, APH answers five questions:

| Question | APH answer | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Who must create a deposit? | New institutions recommended for LoA; existing universities under the stated conditions | 1.12, 4.10 |
| How much? | Table 1.4 by programme/level; 50% concession; leased multiplier | Table 1.4; Addendum Items 10–11 |
| How long? | 10 years; interest retained by AICTE; principal returned | 1.12(iv)–(v) |
| What if late / encashed early / closed? | 10%/20% late; 10% early; 10% or 50% closure | 2.25(f), 7.7, 2.21(h)–(i) |
| How is it released? | Documents + PGRC/Vigilance/Grants clearances + NOC/sanction | 6.46 |

`[FACT]` The **Addendum/Corrigendum 2026-27** is a separate 21-page amendment. It does not replace APH; it changes specific clauses. For SIH26265: the 50% concession (Item 10), the leased-building/land multiplier (Item 11), the late-deposit cross-reference (Item 28), and RPGF in release (Items 36–37). We encode APH and the addendum as two versioned rule packs.

`[INFERENCE]` That is why the product is a policy-native control tower, not an upload portal: the hard part is knowing which version of which clause applies to this institution, this instrument, this date.

## 4.1 Who pays

`[FACT]` Applicants for starting new technical institutions (other than Government/Government-aided) recommended for LoA by the Executive Committee create a security deposit. (Clause 1.12)

`[FACT]` Existing institutions that applied under Clause 1.3.3 and have existed for more than 5 years with the respective regulatory bodies are exempt. (Clause 1.12(i))

`[FACT]` Applicants under Clause 1.5.2 recommended for starting new technical institutions create the deposit for the balance amount. (Clause 1.12(ii))

`[FACT]` A deposit created earlier is for the remaining period of 10 years, as applicable. No deposit is required where an NOC was already issued for release of the earlier FDR. (Clause 1.12(iii))

`[FACT]` Central University/State University/Government Deemed University are not required to pay. (Clause 4.10(a))

`[FACT]` Private Universities/Private Deemed Universities in existence for more than 10 years with UGC are exempt; otherwise they pay for 10 years per Table 1.4. (Clause 4.10(b))

`[FACT]` If any University starts a new Programme/Level, it must create the security deposit per APH even if it has existed for more than 10 years with UGC. (Clause 4.10(c))

|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Engineering and Technology | 15 | 35 | 15 |
| 2 | Planning | 15 | 15 | 15 |

`[FACT]` The 2026-27 addendum changes the note to “DOUBLE in case of leased building / land” — it removes “private.”

## 4.3 The 2026-27 concession

`[FACT]` Addendum/Corrigendum 2026-27, Item 10:

> “1.12. Table 1.4: Security Deposit for New Institutions established / Existing institutions applying for New Level / New Programme in North Eastern States/Jammu & Kashmir/Ladakh / Aspirational Districts / Andaman and Nicobar / Lakshadweep / only Women Institutions / Institutions only for PwD shall be 50% as mentioned in Table 1.4.”

This is the single clearest gap in the incumbent repos.

## 4.4 The 10-year hold and interest

`[FACT]` The amount remains with the Council for **10 years**. (Clause 1.12(iv))

`[FACT]` The interest accumulated is **retained and utilized by the Council** for institutional development, quality improvement, faculty scholarships, etc. (Clause 1.12(iv))

`[FACT]` The **principal amount** is returned to the Trust/Society/Company on completion of the term. The term may be extended for a further period on a case-by-case basis and/or forfeited for violation of norms/conditions/requirements, non-performance, or complaints. (Clause 1.12(v))

**Product implication:** track principal and interest separately. Never include interest in the refundable amount. Show the 10-year clock.

## 4.5 Penalties

| Situation | Penalty | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Late deposit up to 31 May | 10% of security deposit | Clause 2.25(f) |
| Late deposit up to 31 July | 20% of security deposit | Clause 2.25(f) |
| Beyond 31 July | Approval withdrawn | Clause 2.25(f) |
| FDR encashed before maturity / not deposited at LoA | 10% of FDR value | Clause 7.7 |
| Closure release | 10% of security deposit | Clause 2.21(h) |
| No EoA application for preceding six years and AICTE closes | 50% of security deposit | Clause 2.21(i) |
| Extension / forfeiture | Competent-authority discretion | Clause 1.12(v) |

`[FACT]` The 2026-27 addendum Item 28 confirms the 10%/20% late-deposit penalty and changes the cross-reference from Clause 2.3.2 to Clause 2.12.

**Product implication:** the penalty engine shows the rule and the amount, but adverse action requires human approval. Do not auto-penalize.

## 4.6 Release under Clause 6.46

`[FACT]` The Trust/Society/Company submits:

1. Application/request letter for FDR/RTGS release.
2. Affidavit 6 with institution/Trust/Society/Company and FDR/RTGS details.
3. Copy of FDR to be released, RPGF corpus proof, copies of Demand Draft (if any) submitted for RPGF / online payment proof for the security deposit.
4. Copy of the latest LoA/EoA.

`[FACT]` Clearance from **PGRC, Vigilance Cell and Other Cells/Bureaus (Grants released to the Institutions)** is required. (Clause 6.46(b))

`[FACT]` For FDR/RPGF, after all clearances, an **NOC** is issued by the Approval Bureau. (Clause 6.46(c))

`[FACT]` For RTGS, the **sanction order** is issued by the Council. (Clause 6.46(d))

`[FACT]` If the institution has financial embezzlement with Government bodies/banks, the deposit is **not released** until NOC from that body is received. (Clause 6.46(e))

`[FACT]` Violation of security-deposit norms → appropriate penal action. (Clause 6.46(f))

`[FACT]` For AICTE-approved institutions later converted into a Private University/Deemed University, release is subject to **no pending complaints or disciplinary proceedings**. (Clause 6.46(g))

`[FACT]` For progressive closure, release on request subject to a certificate from the affiliating University/Board that no students are studying; **not needed if the deposit is already matured**. (Clause 6.46(h))

`[FACT]` For merger, the child institution’s deposit is released on request subject to the same “no students” certificate. (Clause 6.46(i))

**Official vs. system-level:** Clause 6.46 explicitly names the document set, PGRC, Vigilance, other cells/bureaus (grants), financial-embezzlement NOC, converted-university complaints/disciplinary proceedings, progressive-closure certificate, merger certificate, and RTGS vs. FDR/RPGF routing. “Outstanding dues/penalties” and “bank lien/discharge” are system-level controls implied by Clause 6.46(f) and Clause 1.12; label them as system checks, not APH-mandated clearances, until AICTE confirms.

**Official AICTE circular lead**

- `[UNVERIFIED]` AICTE’s 2026-27 approval page (`https://aicte.gov.in/approal-process-2026-2027`) and `https://aicte.gov.in/node/3299` index a circular titled “Release of FDR / Security Deposit Upon Completion of Maturity Period -reg” (listed 6 October 2025). A secondary Scribd copy summarizes that institutions with matured FDRs must apply through the AICTE portal, and legal/compliance issues block release until resolved. This directly supports the PS pain. The official PDF returned 404 to our fetcher on 25 Oct 2026; do not quote it as verified until downloaded.

## 4.7 RPGF

`[FACT]` RPGF = **Refundable Performance Guaranty Fund**.

`[FACT]` Clause 6.46 treats RPGF alongside FDR/RTGS for release. The 2026-27 addendum explicitly adds RPGF to the release heading and application/Affidavit 6 language.

**Product implication:** support at least four instrument families: **FDR**, **RTGS/online security deposit**, **RPGF**, and optionally **Bank Guarantee (BG)**.

## 4.8 Requirement formula

```
base = Table1_4[programme][level]

if location_category in {NE, J&K, Ladakh, Aspirational District, A&N, Lakshadweep}
   or institution_type in {women_only, PwD_only}:
    base = base * 0.50          # Addendum 2026-27, Item 10

if building_or_land == leased:
    base = base * 2.00          # Addendum 2026-27, Item 11

if exemption applies:
    required = 0
else if existing_deposit and remaining_term:
    required = max(0, base - eligible_existing_credit)
else:
    required = base
```

**Worked examples**

| Case | Base | Concession | Leased | Required |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Engineering UG, owned, co-ed | 35L | — | — | 35L |
| Engineering UG, leased, co-ed | 35L | — | ×2 | 70L |
| Engineering UG, owned, NE | 35L | ×0.5 | — | 17.5L |
| Engineering UG, leased, NE | 35L | ×0.5 | ×2 | 35L |
| Engineering UG, leased, women-only, NE | 35L | ×0.5 | ×2 | 35L |
| Management UG, owned | 5L | — | — | 5L |
| Engineering Diploma, owned | 15L | — | — | 15L |

**Policy trace UI must show:** base → concession → leased multiplier → exemption/credit → required, with clause citations and effective dates.

## 4.9 Exemptions

- Government/Government-aided.
- Central University/State University/Government Deemed University.
- Existing >5 years with respective regulatory bodies.
- Private University/Private Deemed University >10 years with UGC.
- NOC already issued for earlier FDR.
- New Programme/Level at a >10-year university: **not exempt** for that new programme/level.

## 4.10 Edge cases the policy engine must handle

- Leased + NE + women-only: 35L → 17.5L → 35L.
- Existing deposit partially covers the new requirement.
- Existing deposit for the remaining 10-year term.
- Instrument type RPGF vs. FDR vs. RTGS.
- Policy effective date: base APH vs. 2026-27 addendum.
- NOC already issued for an earlier FDR: no new deposit.
- University starts a new programme/level: deposit applies even if >10 years with UGC.
- Closure penalty 10% vs. 50% depending on the six-year non-application condition.
- Matured deposit: progressive-closure “no students” certificate not needed.

# 6. What we are building — module by module

## M1. Institution 360 and Instrument Register

**Institution master**

- AICTE Permanent ID, name, Trust/Society/Company, region, state, district.
- Location category: NE, J&K, Ladakh, Aspirational District, A&N, Lakshadweep, Other.
- Institution type: co-ed, women-only, PwD-only.
- Building/land: owned or leased.
- University type: affiliated, private university, deemed, government.
- Established year, years with UGC, approval status.

**Instrument record**

- Type: FDR, RTGS, RPGF, BG.
- Instrument number, bank, branch, IFSC.
- Amount, issue date, maturity date, tenure, interest rate.
- Depositor name, lien in favour of AICTE.
- Status, document IDs, bank verification IDs.
- Predecessor/successor for renewal lineage.
- Current administrative status.

**Status machine**

```
DRAFT
  → SUBMITTED
  → UNDER_SCRUTINY
  → QUERY_RAISED
  → VERIFIED
  → ACTIVE
  → EXPIRING
  → MATURED
  → RENEWAL_DUE
  → RENEWED / REPLACED
  → RELEASE_REQUESTED
  → CLEARANCES_PENDING
  → APPROVED
  → RELEASED / DISCHARGED
  → CLOSED

Side exits: REJECTED, WITHDRAWN, FORFEITED
```

Every transition writes an audit event and, where relevant, an obligation.

## M2. Intake, OCR and three-way provenance

**Intake**

- Upload PDF/image.
- SHA-256 fingerprint.
- AES-256-GCM at rest.
- Versioning; no overwrite.
- Text-layer extraction first; OCR fallback.
- Human review for low-confidence fields.

**Three values per key field**

1. **Declared** — what the institution entered.
2. **Document** — what the FDR/document says; page/bbox/confidence.
3. **Bank** — what the bank verification response says; source/reference/timestamp.

**Discrepancy rules**

| Rule | Severity |
|---|---|
| Amount mismatch | Critical |
| Instrument number mismatch | Critical |
| Issue/maturity date mismatch | Serious |
| IFSC/branch/bank mismatch | Serious |
| Depositor ≠ Trust/Society/Company | Serious |
| Lien not in favour of AICTE | Critical |
| Tenure < required | Serious |
| Duplicate instrument number across institutions | Critical |
| Duplicate file hash | Critical |
| Missing prescribed documents | Warning |
| Bank record not found/encashed/closed | Critical |

Every finding has:

- severity,
- reason,
- evidence pointer (document/page/bbox),
- suggested action,
- human disposition: `open`, `accepted`, `waived` with reason, `resolved`.

## M4. Bank Verification Adapter

**Interface**

```
verify(instrument) -> {
  status: FOUND | NOT_FOUND | ENCASHED | CLOSED | UNSUPPORTED | SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE,
  record: { amount, issue_date, maturity_date, ifsc, branch, depositor, lien },
  source, reference, timestamp, confidence
}
```

**Modes**

1. **API** — where authorized.
2. **Bank letter/e-mail** — upload + officer-recorded fields.
3. **Sandbox** — demo.

**Rules**

- Never auto-accept without bank confirmation unless a Director/Competent Authority records a written reason.
- Label the record `officer-verified`, not `bank-confirmed`.
- Re-verify every 30 days to catch post-acceptance encashment.
- `NOT_FOUND` / `SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` → manual review; never auto-reject.

## M5. Lifecycle, reminders and time machine

**Daily idempotent job**

- Move to `MATURED` on maturity date.
- Reminder ladder T-90/60/30/15/7/0 while renewal is needed.
- Overdue notice every 7 days after maturity.
- Escalation: L1 dealing officer (day 1), L2 Director (day 15), L3 Competent Authority with final notice (day 30).
- Periodic bank re-check.
- Auto-resolve alerts when the condition clears.

**Time machine**

- Advance the system date.
- Show reminders, maturity, escalation, renewal live.
- Never mutate production data; demo clock is separate.

**Renewal lineage**

- Successor instrument linked to predecessor.
- Gap/overlap detection.
- History preserved.

## M6. Release Eligibility Graph

A release request is a node; the graph evaluates:

**Official Clause 6.46 nodes**

- Document set complete: application, Affidavit 6, FDR/RPGF proof, latest LoA/EoA.
- PGRC clearance.
- Vigilance clearance.
- Grants/other cell clearance.
- Financial embezzlement NOC (if applicable).
- Pending complaints/disciplinary proceedings (converted universities).
- Progressive-closure “no students” certificate (not needed if matured).
- Merger “no students” certificate.
- RTGS vs. FDR/RPGF routing: sanction order vs. NOC.

**System-level controls**

- Term served / 10-year clock.
- Outstanding dues/penalties.
- Bank lien/discharge confirmation.

Label system-level controls as system checks, not APH-mandated clearances.

**Output**

- `ELIGIBLE`
- `BLOCKED` with blocker nodes
- `NEEDS_INFO`
- `PENDING_SOURCE`

**Workflow**

```
Maker (dealing officer)
  → Checker (Director)
  → Approver (Competent Authority)
  → Accounts (release letter/refund)
```

Segregation of duties enforced. Every decision cites the clause and the evidence.

## M7. Pledge Graph and Fraud Detection

**Graph nodes**

- Institutions, instruments, banks, approvals, documents, signatories.

**Edges**

- pledged for,
- renewed from,
- same instrument number,
- same file hash,
- same account,
- same signatory.

**Detections**

- One FDR pledged for two institutions/approvals.
- One file re-used with altered metadata.
- Bank branch/IFSC does not match the instrument.
- Premature encashment after acceptance.
- Maturity date arithmetic inconsistent with issue date + tenure.
- Interest rate/amount anomalies.

Use graph algorithms (connected components, shared-neighbour scoring) but keep the output explainable.

## M8. Maturity Cash-Flow Forecast

Portfolio view for the Director/Competent Authority:

- ₹ maturing by quarter/month.
- Expected renewals vs. releases.
- Instruments with no renewal instruction.
- At-risk amount (critical findings + lapsed + bank-not-found).
- Staffing load (verification/release requests per officer).
- Region/bank/programme concentration.

Export CSV/PDF; drill-down to the filtered list.

This is a genuine differentiator: most teams stop at “active/expiring/matured” counts.

## M9. Evidence Vault, Audit and Security

- Document store: AES-256-GCM at rest; SHA-256 fingerprints; signed short-lived URLs; no public buckets.
- Audit: append-only, hash-chained:
  `SHA256(prev_hash + event + actor + timestamp + payload)`.
- Verify endpoint; export.

# 7. End-to-end workflows

## 7.1 New FDR submission and verification

1. Institution logs in; selects institution/programme/level.
2. System computes requirement from Policy Pack.
3. Institution enters instrument details (declared).
4. Institution uploads FDR and supporting documents.
5. System extracts fields (document), computes SHA-256, stores encrypted.
6. Dealing officer reviews.
7. Bank adapter returns bank record or `SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`.
8. System shows three-way provenance and findings.
9. Officer dispositions findings; query raised if needed.
10. Instrument becomes `VERIFIED` → `ACTIVE`.
11. Audit event recorded.

## 7.2 Discrepancy resolution

1. Finding is `open`.
2. Institution sees the reason and evidence.
3. Institution responds/uploads corrected document.
4. Officer accepts, waives with reason, or resolves.
5. Critical findings cannot be waived without Director/Competent Authority approval.
6. Audit records both the finding and the disposition.

## 7.3 Maturity and renewal

1. Daily job checks maturity.
2. T-90/60/30/15/7/0 reminders.
3. Institution submits renewal/replacement.
4. New instrument linked to predecessor.
5. Gap/overlap detection.
6. Old instrument `RENEWED`/`REPLACED`; new instrument `ACTIVE`.
7. If no renewal, `MATURED` → overdue notices → escalation.

## 7.4 Release

1. Institution requests release.
2. System checks documents and term.
3. Release graph queries PGRC, Vigilance, Grants, financial embezzlement, complaints/disciplinary, closure/merger, bank lien.
4. Graph returns `BLOCKED` with missing nodes or `ELIGIBLE`.
5. Maker → Checker → Approver.
6. For FDR/RPGF: NOC by Approval Bureau. For RTGS: sanction order by Council.
7. Accounts issues release letter/refund confirmation.
8. Instrument `RELEASED`/`DISCHARGED`; audit event.

## 7.5 Fraud / duplicate pledge

1. Nightly or on-demand graph scan.
2. Detect same instrument number/hash/account across institutions.
3. Create critical finding.
4. Block acceptance/release until resolved.
5. Show graph with explanation.

## 7.6 Audit verification

1. Auditor opens audit log.
2. Clicks “Verify chain.”
3. System recomputes hashes from genesis.
4. Shows valid/invalid at the first broken event.
5. Export CSV/PDF.

## 9.5 Policy engine design

```python
def calculate_requirement(institution, programme, level, pack, existing_credit=0):
    base = pack.table_1_4(programme, level)
    trace = [{"step": "base", "rule": "Table 1.4", "amount": base}]

    if institution.location_category in CONCESSION_LOCATIONS \
       or institution.institution_type in {"WOMEN_ONLY", "PWD_ONLY"}:
        base = base * Decimal("0.50")
        trace.append({"step": "concession", "rule": "Addendum 2026-27 Item 10", "amount": base})

    if institution.building_ownership == "LEASED":
        base = base * Decimal("2.00")
        trace.append({"step": "leased", "rule": "Addendum 2026-27 Item 11", "amount": base})

    if exemption_applies(institution, programme, level, pack):
        trace.append({"step": "exemption", "rule": "...", "amount": 0})
        return 0, trace

    required = max(Decimal("0"), base - existing_credit)
    trace.append({"step": "required", "amount": required})
    return required, trace
```

**Rules**

- Use integer paise, not floats.
- Every step has a clause and source.
- Every result is reproducible from the trace.
- Effective dates select the pack.
- Ambiguous cases route to human review.

## 9.6 Release graph design

```python
def evaluate_release(release_request):
    nodes = build_nodes(release_request)
    blockers = [n for n in nodes if n.required and n.status != "CLEAR"]
    if any(n.status == "SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE" for n in nodes):
        return "PENDING_SOURCE", blockers
    if blockers:
        return "BLOCKED", blockers
    if any(n.status == "NEEDS_INFO" for n in nodes):
        return "NEEDS_INFO", blockers
    return "ELIGIBLE", []
```

**Rules**

- Official nodes and system nodes are typed.
- Critical blockers cannot be overridden by the maker.
- The approver sees the exact missing node and source.
- Every clearance has a reference and timestamp.
- The graph is recomputed after each clearance.

## 9.7 Audit chain

```python
def append_event(event):
    prev = last_event_hash()
    payload = canonical_json(event)
    digest = sha256(prev + payload + event.actor + event.timestamp)
    insert(seq=next_seq(), prev_hash=prev, hash=digest, payload=payload)
```

**Rules**

- Append-only.
- No updates/deletes.
- Verify from genesis.
- Export includes hashes.
- A tamper test must break the chain visibly.

# 17. Jury Q&A

1. **“Why not just use Excel or an ERP?”**
   Excel cannot prove provenance, enforce release clearances, or produce a tamper-evident audit. An ERP stores records; SURAKSHA enforces the APH rules and shows the evidence.

2. **“This already exists. Why yours?”**
   Existing tools are generic document/workflow products. SURAKSHA is built around the AICTE security-deposit lifecycle: Table 1.4, the 2026-27 50% concession, the 10-year hold, the 6.46 clearance graph, and the ₹ at-risk portfolio.

3. **“Will banks give you APIs?”**
   Not universally. We designed a bank verification adapter with three modes: authorized API, bank letter/e-mail confirmation, and a labelled sandbox. Production access requires an MoU; the prototype is honest about that.

4. **“Why not blockchain?”**
   A hash-chained audit log gives tamper-evidence without consensus overhead. Blockchain could later notarize across organizations, but it is not required for the core problem.

5. **“How do you handle the 2026-27 addendum?”**
   The Policy Pack is versioned. The calculator applies the 50% concession for NE/J&K/Ladakh/Aspirational Districts/A&N/Lakshadweep/women-only/PwD-only institutions, doubles for leased building/land, and includes RPGF in release. The policy diff screen shows base vs. addendum.

6. **“What about interest?”**
   APH says the interest accumulated is retained by AICTE; only the principal is returned. We track principal and interest separately and never include interest in the refundable amount.

7. **“What if the FDR is encashed early?”**
   The 30-day bank re-check flags it as critical; the system raises a show-cause and applies the 10% penalty under Clause 7.7, with human approval.

8. **“How do you prevent one FDR being used twice?”**
   Duplicate instrument number, duplicate file hash, bank confirmation, and the pledge graph. Critical findings block acceptance.

9. **“How do you handle release when a complaint is pending?”**
   The release eligibility graph queries PGRC, Vigilance, Grants, dues, and disciplinary proceedings. It blocks release and shows the exact missing clearance.

10. **“What if the bank says ‘not found’?”**
    Status `NOT_FOUND` or `SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`; route to manual review. We never auto-reject on a bank non-response.

11. **“Is this scalable?”**
    Postgres + object storage + async jobs; region/bureau scoping; 10-year records; indexes on institution/instrument/maturity; audit partitioning. The demo is small, the design is not.

12. **“What is your moat?”**
    The Policy Pack + release graph + provenance + portfolio forecast. The UI is easy to copy; the regulatory encoding and evidence trail are not.

13. **“Why will institutions use it?”**
    One window, clear status, fewer queries, reminders, faster release.

14. **“Why will AICTE use it?”**
    ₹ at-risk visibility, release control, audit, workload management, and a defensible decision record.

15. **“What is simulated?”**
    Bank verification, AICTE approval data, complaints, DigiLocker/eSign, APAAR/ABC, payments. Everything is labelled. The policy engine, workflow, audit, and UI are real.

16. **“What about data privacy?”**
    Role-based access, field-level encryption for account numbers, encrypted documents, masked PII, audit logs, retention policy. DPDP alignment is a design goal, not a certification claim.

17. **“What if the institution disputes a finding?”**
    Every finding has evidence and a disposition; the institution can respond; the officer can waive with a reason (except critical findings); the audit records both.

18. **“What happens after the 10-year term?”**
    The system computes maturity, checks release eligibility, and routes to NOC/sanction. Extension/forfeiture is a competent-authority decision, not automatic.

19. **“How is this different from the strongest GitHub repo?”**
    They built a strong portal with provenance and reminders. We add the exact 2026-27 policy, the release eligibility graph, the pledge graph, and the maturity cash-flow forecast. We respect their work; we are building the compliance brain.

20. **“Can you show it in 5 minutes?”**
    Yes: submit → provenance mismatch → policy trace → release blocked → clearance → time machine → audit verify.

21. **“Isn’t this just CRUD?”**
    A CRUD portal stores records. SURAKSHA has a versioned policy engine, three-way provenance, a 6.46 release graph, a time machine, a pledge graph, a cash-flow forecast, and a hash-chained audit. The complexity is in the rules, evidence, time and release logic — not the UI.

22. **“Why not choose the OCR/forgery PS?”**
    That field already has multiple strong teams with OCR, ELA, trust scores, tests and live demos. We chose the PS where we can be more accurate than the field, not the one where we can be the 14th dashboard.
